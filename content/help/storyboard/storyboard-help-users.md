# Storyboard

A Storyboard is where you give a longer piece of Writing its shape — Parts, Chapters, and Scenes, arranged in the order a reader will encounter them. Today, Storyboard supports fiction: novels, novellas, and other scene-based work.

## What you can do here

- Create a new Storyboard and give it a working title
- Build structure by adding Parts and Chapters
- Add Scenes under a Chapter — each Scene is a real Writing piece you can open and write in directly
- Reorder Parts, Chapters, and Scenes within their siblings
- Select any Scene to write or edit its prose in place

## Key concepts

**Storyboard** — The overall arrangement for one piece of long-form Writing. It doesn't hold prose itself; it holds the order.

**Grammar** — The set of rules for what can nest under what. Fiction's grammar is Part → Chapter → Scene: a Chapter can stand on its own without a Part, but a Scene always needs a Chapter.

**Scene** — The only level in a Storyboard with actual prose. Selecting a Scene opens the same editor used everywhere else in Mixtape for writing a piece — there's only one place your words live, the Storyboard just arranges it.

## How to build a Storyboard

1. Open **Storyboard** from the navigation and select **Create Storyboard**, optionally giving it a working title.
2. Add a **Part** or **Chapter** from the toolbar at the top.
3. Under a Chapter, use **+ Scene** to add a new Scene, then select it to start writing.
4. Use the up/down arrows next to any Part, Chapter, or Scene to reorder it among its siblings.

## What this build does not do yet

This is an early build of the Storyboard spine. It does not yet show a spatial/visual board view, support Characters or other participants, or let you attach Folio Notes to a specific Part, Chapter, or Scene — those are planned for later phases.
