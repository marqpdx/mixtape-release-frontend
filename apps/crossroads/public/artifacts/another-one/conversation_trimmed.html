  • No shared responsibility

Let’s name the distinction explicitly

There are (at least) three different things people mean by “member”, and Mixtape needs to tease them apart:

1. Contractual belonging

I have opted into a formal system with rights, duties, and continuity.

3. Participatory involvement

I take part in activities here.

This is looser, often temporary.

English collapses all three into member.
Mixtape should not.

The real question underneath your question

When someone is invited into Group A, are they also made a de facto member of Crossroads?

This isn’t just a UX decision. It’s about whether Crossroads is a prerequisite commons or merely an account substrate.

There are two coherent answers. One is cleaner long-term. The other is more permissive short-term.

Let’s walk both honestly.

Option 1: Group invitation ⇒ Crossroads Membership (strong default)

Interpretation

You cannot meaningfully belong to anything inside Crossroads without belonging to Crossroads itself.

So:

  • Group invitation triggers (or requires) becoming a Crossroads Member

  • Crossroads is the civic wrapper that makes Groups possible

  • Groups are sub-structures, not independent islands

What this buys you

1. Conceptual integrity

  • One commons

  • One social contract

  • One shared baseline of norms

People don’t just “drop into” a Community — they enter a place first.

2. Clean semantics

  • “Member” means something, once

  • Groups don’t accidentally become alternative roots

  • Future federation is saner

3. Economic clarity

  • Crossroads membership is the base layer

  • Group fees (later) are additive, not substitutive

  • You avoid the nightmare of people “inside Groups but not the system”

4. Governance sanity

  • Moderation, trust, identity, and safety live at the Crossroads layer

  • Groups don’t have to reinvent civic rules

The cost

Friction.
You are saying:

“To be part of anything here, you step into the commons.”

That’s philosophically strong — but you have to be comfortable with it.

Option 2: Group-first entry, Crossroads optional (looser, but leaky)

Interpretation

Someone can be part of a Group without fully joining Crossroads.

So:

  • You can be invited into Group A

  • You may later become a Crossroads Member

  • Crossroads acts more like an account + service layer

What this buys you

1. Lower barrier

  • Easier invites

  • Fewer conceptual steps

  • Feels more like Slack / Discord / Substack hybrids

2. Matches some real-world flows

  • “Come to this workshop”

  • “Join this cohort”

  • “Be part of this thing” without broader commitment

The cost (and this is important)

Semantic erosion

  • Crossroads stops being a place

  • Membership becomes fuzzy

  • Groups become de facto roots

Future pain

  • Paid Crossroads membership becomes harder to justify

  • Governance becomes fragmented

  • “Why am I paying for Crossroads if I only care about Group X?”

You end up rebuilding the commons later — badly.

The third way (this is the interesting one)

You don’t actually have to choose binary.

You can distinguish between:

Presence vs Belonging

Here’s the key move:

  • Anyone can be present (invited, visiting, participating)

  • Only some are Members (of Crossroads)

This gives you a ladder instead of a gate.

How this would work conceptually

When someone is invited into Group A:

  • They get a temporary, scoped presence

  • They are part of the Group

  • They are not yet a Crossroads Member

But:

  • Certain actions require membership

  • Continued participation nudges toward joining

  • Belonging deepens over time

This mirrors real life:

You can attend a gathering before you join the organization.

But — and this is crucial — the language must stay clear

You would say:

  • “You’re invited into this Community.”

  • “To become a Crossroads Member, join the commons.”

Not:

  • “You’re already a member.”

This preserves the semantic weight of Member.

Where LanternMail fits perfectly here

LanternMail already establishes:

  • presence without belonging

  • attention without obligation

So you have a gradient:

sql
Subscriber → Participant → Crossroads Member

Groups can span the middle band without collapsing the top.

On nouns like Kith, Fellow, Apprentice

You’re making an important clarification:

We don’t attach system meaning to these nouns.

Yes. Exactly.

They are:

  • labels

  • self-descriptions

  • cultural markers

Not permission tiers. Not economic contracts.

This is healthy.

It also means:

  • groups own their language

  • Mixtape doesn’t freeze culture into schema

  • future renaming doesn’t require migrations

So, back to your question — my honest take

If you forced me to choose today:

I would say:

  • Crossroads Membership is required for durable participation

  • But not for initial entry

In other words:

  • You can enter via a Group

  • You belong via Crossroads

That preserves:

  • invitation-based flows

  • real-world onboarding patterns

  • the integrity of the commons

And it lets you say, truthfully:

“You don’t have to commit immediately — but if you stay, we ask you to join.”

That’s not extractive. It’s respectful.

  2. Do you want Groups to feel like doorways — or destinations?

Your answer to those will snap this into focus.
