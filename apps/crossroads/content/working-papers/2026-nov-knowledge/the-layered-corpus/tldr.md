summary: 'As AI makes code cheaper to produce, the capacity it frees is best spent on knowledge: five layers (code, how it''s built, how it''s checked, what''s intended, what''s being learned), each IP in its own right. Capture is the bottleneck.'
---

Code once took most of a technology budget; AI-assisted building frees much of that. Most teams will spend it on more code. The paper argues for spending it on the knowledge around the code, in five layers:

1. **Code**, still the definitive core;
2. **Orchestrator**: plans, standards, agent instructions;
3. **Audit and CI**: whether the code and the layers above it still tell the truth;
4. **Initiatives**: ideas in flight;
5. **Exploration**: curation, the team's own body of precedent.

Value compounds where the layers link. Capture is the bottleneck; machines can propose links, people decide which are true. It closes with a stress test, two case studies (a school, a legal collective) and notes from running many agents at once. It builds on Nonaka and Takeuchi's knowledge spiral.

**Names:** *Initiative* is a stretch of purposeful work in Mixtape; *Ariadne* is its inquiry engine.
