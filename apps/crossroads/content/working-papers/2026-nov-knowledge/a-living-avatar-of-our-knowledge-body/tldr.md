Does a body of knowledge only grow larger as we work, or can it become **more capable of knowing**? The paper's answer turns on coherence: knowledge becomes more usable when relationships are recognizable, provenance recoverable and contradictions examinable, sometimes richer *because there is less in it*.

It braids two strands:

- **Patterns of living systems:** metabolism, memory, discernment, repair, adaptation, release.
- **Practices of tending:** water, prune, graft, transplant, compost, mend, harvest, leave fallow.

It proposes keepers that notice, investigate and propose while people decide. It is explicit that the biology is analogy: Mixtape is not trying to become an organism. A first test is proposed: can ordinary tending measurably improve a knowledge body's health?
